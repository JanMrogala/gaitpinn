Method

Question

Two models are trained on level walking only. Each is then fed a stream that switches from walking to stair climbing, with no retraining. Which model keeps a low joint-angle error for longer after the switch?

Name What it is
base small causal network, trained on angle error only (data-only)
pinn the same network, trained on angle error plus a physics loss

Main result: a plot of error over time since the switch, with base and pinn on the same axes.

Data

Several datasets meet needs 1–5 on paper: Camargo, Darmstadt and Scherpereel, the last except for height. Camargo is the strongest of them because it is the only one with all of these:

  • Dedicated IMUs on thigh, shank and foot. Darmstadt has no dedicated thigh IMU; Scherpereel has no dedicated segment IMUs at all.
  • Plenty of steady walking for training. Treadmill at 28 speeds plus level-ground circuits, against short, non-steady bouts in Darmstadt and 3 treadmill speeds in Scherpereel.
  • The most people: 22, against 12 for each of the other two.
  • About 40 stair files per person.
  • 11 EMG channels, with three knee extensors and four knee flexors.

Caveats (to handle in the data step)

  • One session per person, so session-to-session drift can't be tested with Camargo. MyPredict 3 is the candidate for that later.
  • Sensors on the right leg only. The target is the right hip, knee and ankle (*_r columns in the IK table).
  • The gyro axis and sign differ per person. The best axis for d(knee)/dt ≈ gyro_shank − gyro_thigh is Y in one person and a Y/Z mix in others (3 people checked). Alignment must be fitted per person on walking training data.
  • Stair files are short. Each is ~15 s and contains:
  • a 2–3 s walking approach;
  • an ascent of 1.3–3 s;
  • a 3–6 s stand and turn on the top platform;
  • a descent of 1.2–2.8 s;
  • about one stride of walk-off.

The files are cut from longer recordings, and the 3–8 s between repetitions are not released. - The idle label at the start of stair files is already walking. Use pelvis speed or gait events, not labels, to separate walking from standing. - The files are MATLAB tables, which scipy can't read directly. They need a one-off conversion. - EMG is band-passed raw (20–400 Hz). It must be rectified, smoothed with causal filters and resampled.

Metrics

All errors are RMSE in degrees, per person, model, seed and joint.

  • Rolling error: RMSE over a trailing 2 s window.
  • Walking error W: RMSE over the person's level-ground walking test files. This matches the walking part of the stream. One W is used for both the dashed line and the failure threshold. The RMSE over the whole walking test split, treadmill included, is reported separately.
  • Error after switch: RMSE over the stairs part.
  • Time to fail:
  • Computed per seed: the first t ≥ 0 at which the rolling error rises above 1.5 × W and stays above it for the next 2 s.
  • A hold that runs past the end of the stream counts as not failed.
  • Censoring: a run that never fails is set to H, the stairs-part length of the person with the shortest stream. All time-to-fail values are also capped at H, so every person has the same horizon. A run that never fails is reported as "> H".
  • Per person: the median over the 5 seeds.
  • Seeds: every other metric is averaged over the 5 seeds per person first, so each person counts once.

Plots

Main plot (error_over_time):

  • x = time since switch (s), from −30 s to H; y = rolling error (°).
  • One panel each for hip, knee and ankle.
  • Lines for base and pinn: the mean over people of the seed-averaged rolling error.
  • The band is the t-based 95% confidence interval of that mean across people.
  • A vertical line at t = 0 and a dashed line at each model's mean W.

Secondary plot (error_by_bout):

  • Error against time since each stair entry, plotted separately for ascent and descent.
  • Entries are the first sample labelled walk-stairascent or walk-stairdescent.
  • The window runs from −2 s to +6 s.
  • Formula: at each offset, sqrt(mean over bouts of e²) per person, then the mean over people.
  • Labels are used only to align the scoring, never as model inputs.
  • In step 4, the descent entry label is checked on the label tables only, before any model is trained.

Comparison and verdict

  • Primary endpoint: error after switch, averaged over hip, knee and ankle. The question is about all three joint angles. Hip has no physics term, so a knee-only endpoint would tilt the test towards pinn.
  • Test: a paired, two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank test across included people, on base − pinn, with zero_method="wilcox".
  • Effect size: the matched-pairs rank-biserial correlation r, plus the Hodges–Lehmann estimate of base − pinn in degrees with a 95% bootstrap interval.
  • Verdict:
  • "pinn is better": p < 0.05 and r > 0.
  • "base is better": p < 0.05 and r < 0.
  • Otherwise "no clear difference".
  • All three outcomes are reported equally clearly.
  • Secondary results (reported, not part of the verdict):
  • error after switch per joint;
  • error after switch on ascent and on descent samples only;
  • time to fail per joint (same paired test, using the capped values);
  • the error_by_bout curves.

Hip linear acceleration is neglected. - The foot centre of mass sits at the ankle (Winter tables otherwise). - Muscles: BF, ST, gracilis and MG are flexors (+); VM, VL and RF are extensors (−). TA, soleus, glut. med. and the oblique are not used. - Activation: envelope / its 95th percentile on walking training files, then a 40 ms first-order filter. - Swing: foot gyro norm above its 60th percentile on walking training files, so about 40% of walking samples count as swing, for at least 100 ms. - 2026-11-19, gyro alignment, final form (walking data only; no model trained yet). With the principal-axis alignment, 14 of 22 people passed the required gyro check. For each person and joint there are now two candidates, both fitted on walking training files: - pca: each sensor's principal gyro axis; - lsq: each sensor's unit axis from a least-squares fit to the true joint speed.

So each array task trains one (model, person) pair on 1 GCD: either all 12 tuning configurations or the 5 final seeds, one after another. That gives 44 tasks per batch. Each run still uses exactly one GCD. - 2026-11-19, local AB25 development runs. Two 2-epoch runs on AB25, used to test the code, produced test-stream predictions. Their stair numbers were never printed or looked at, and the runs were deleted. - 2026-11-19, tuning round 2 (declared before it runs; walking validation only, no stair data seen). Round 1 (planned grid) chose base c08 (2.41°) and pinn c03 (λ = 0.03, 3.76°). pinn got worse as λ grew, and a walking-only check on AB25 showed why: - Loss scale: the physics terms sit at about 2.6 even with the true angles (gyro 0.12, swing 2.57). base's converged angle loss is about 0.015, so λ ≥ 0.03 lets physics dominate. - Dropout: with dropout on, the physics terms were 1.6–2× larger, because they differentiate the output in time. - Epoch cap: pinn was still improving at the 60-epoch cap; base stops near 28.

Round 2 uses the same settings for both models: dropout 0 and a cap of 150 epochs. Each model gets 12 new configurations: - base: the same grid as before; - pinn: λ ∈ {0.001, 0.003, 0.01}, so that λ × the floor spans about 0.1–2× the angle loss.

Only round-2 runs are used for selection. This is the last tuning round: if the smallest λ wins, it is used as is. Both rounds count towards the GPU budget and are reported. - 2026-11-19, swing-model fit. With the true angles, the swing residual is about 2.6 times the std of I·knee_acc. That is above what a trained model reaches, so the simple pendulum in the task spec explains true swing motion poorly. Reported as a limitation, not changed. - 2026-11-19, tuning result (round 2, walking validation, 16 included people). - base: r2c07 (lr 3e-3, width 32, weight decay 1e-5), mean validation RMSE 2.30°. - pinn: r2c09 (lr 3e-3, width 64, λ = 0.001), mean validation RMSE 2.34°. λ = 0.001 is the smallest value in the grid; as declared above, it is used as is. The plan is frozen from here: no changes after the final batch. - 2026-11-19, added after the main result, at the user's request: a strongly physics-constrained model, pinn_strong. This is an addition. The pre-registered base vs pinn verdict above is unchanged. - Settings: the same network and settings as the tuned pinn (lr 3e-3, width 64, dropout 0, up to 150 epochs) with a fixed, untuned physics weight λ = 0.1. - Why 0.1: this value was fixed after the base-vs-pinn stairs result was known, but only from walking-only numbers measured before the final batch: the floor of the physics terms with the true angles ≈ 2.6, and base's converged angle loss ≈ 0.015. - At λ = 0.1 the physics term is about 17× the angle loss at the floor, so physics dominates training. - Round 1 had already trained λ = 0.03, 0.3 and 3 on walking validation: pinn got worse as λ grew (λ = 0.03: 3.76° vs base 2.41°, under round-1 settings). 0.1 sits between the two lower values: strongly constrained, without the very large walking errors of λ ≥ 0.3. - No stairs number was used to choose it. - Test: the same as the primary one, on the same 16 people: Wilcoxon on base − pinn_strong, error after switch averaged over the three joints, with the same verdict rule. Reported as a separate, clearly labelled result. - Also reported: its walking-validation and walking-test errors. Its time to fail is shown only as a secondary result, because the threshold is relative to each model's own walking error.
